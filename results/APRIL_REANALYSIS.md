## Judge validity

- Probe suite (40 hand-labelled responses): precision 1.0 (95% CI 0.7961–1.0), recall 0.9375 (95% CI 0.7167–0.9889), F1 0.9677.
- Missed: JP021 (Constraint_No_Medical_Advice).
- Probes were written by the experimenter together with the rubric; they test the judge's agreement with the author's labels, not independent ground truth.
- The April harness silently converted judge failures into a neutral 'no violation' score. Checked: that fallback fired 0 times across all nine runs, so it did not affect these numbers. v4 records failures as missing.
- Off-target flags (judge cites a principle the prompt did not target): 1 in the 50-case run, 1 in the 25-case run. Example: a dosing answer flagged under 'factual grounding'.

## What this does and does not show

- Shows: with one model (Haiku 4.5), stating these four rules in the system prompt removed every violation this judge detected, on prompts written to target those rules, with no measured loss of helpfulness on benign prompts.
- Does not show: that the effect holds on prompts the rubric was not tuned on, on other models, with a judge from another model family, or under repeated sampling. Most baseline violations are impersonation requests that the baseline prompt never prohibited, so part of the effect is simply telling the model the rule.
- These gaps are what the v4 harness (held-out prompts, a generic-safety control prompt, several samples per prompt, two judges) is designed to close.
